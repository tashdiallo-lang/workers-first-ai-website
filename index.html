Can robotaxis benefit the drivers who built the industry?

Workers First AI is a research and policy project built with input from about 100 rideshare and taxi drivers. This report sets out what would need to be true for autonomous-vehicle service to improve — not undercut — the people currently doing the driving.

Our question

Rideshare and taxi drivers built the passenger market autonomous-vehicle companies now hope to serve.

We start from a clear principle: if robotaxi operations are allowed, they should improve drivers' lives rather than reduce their income or control over their time. This report offers a policy framework designed to make that possible — and is honest about what remains unresolved.

Robotaxi companies are expanding in some markets, while workers and public officials have delayed, limited, or stopped proposals in others — from New York and Atlanta to Washington, D.C. Drivers still perform the work, and public officials are still deciding what happens next.

Our recommendation

Public officials should require robotaxi companies to finance:

1

Protection against lost driver income

Before service begins or expands, backed by an initial payment, guarantees, and a reserve.

2

A meaningful additional benefit

Its form, eligibility, and allocation decided by driver representatives chosen under transparent safeguards.

3

Enforcement with teeth

Company records, independent analysis, and the power to reduce or stop service when requirements aren't met.

What the evidence shows

No rigorous published U.S. study yet isolates how robotaxis affect drivers.

Our review through August 3, 2026 found directional evidence — not proof. That gap is exactly why the framework leads with measurement, not assumptions.

GRIDWISE, Q4 2025

Trips per hour fell faster in AV-active markets

Down 5.3% in AV-active markets vs. 2.6% nationwide; hourly gross pay declined in Los Angeles, Phoenix, and San Francisco while rising nationwide. Directional, not causal.

WUHAN, CHINA · 2026 STUDY

Traditional taxi income fell 10.9% short-run

A difference-in-differences study of 1,040 traditional taxis found a 10.9% short-run drop in daily income after robotaxi introduction — evidence effects can be measured, not proof of the U.S. picture.

OUR CONCLUSION

Measurement should come before scale

We recommend independent, company-record-based analysis before service begins or expands — with automatic triggers when findings show harm.

The framework

Five policy components, built to work together

Developed from driver interviews, a public call for proposals, a twelve-member advisory panel, and financial modeling — not written in the abstract.

1

Measure the effects

Require robotaxi and rideshare company records and independent analysis — before and while service operates.

2

Fund driver protection

An initial payment, guarantees, and a reserve before service begins or expands — held independently of the company.

3

Limit expansion

Preserve enough paid work for human drivers, with public officials retaining power to reduce or stop service.

4

Protect against loss, add a real benefit

Loss protection for every eligible driver, plus a meaningful additional benefit shaped by drivers themselves.

5

Fair rules, real authority

Clear eligibility that recognizes full-time, part-time, and multi-app work — with driver representatives deciding allocation.

In their own words

What drivers told us

Across interviews, a survey, and focus groups in New York, San Francisco, and Atlanta, drivers disagreed about whether robotaxis should operate — but returned again and again to the same four priorities.

“
Every person should be allowed to drive that autonomous vehicle, and not to be replaced.
Asad Iqbal — driver, New York City
“
I don't like it. Maybe I am old-fashioned.
Liakat Ali — driver, New York City, on robots taking over people's jobs
“
Some of us don't want free money. Our dignity is being stripped away by them just throwing us away. We just want to know that we're valued.
Atlanta driver, 2026 focus group
“
