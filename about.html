Preface · What this project is trying to make possible

Working people can help shape technological change — before the terms are already set

Public debates about technology too often proceed without the people whose work will change. Experts debate whether jobs will disappear. Companies describe new possibilities. Workers are told to prepare. By the time the evidence is conclusive, the decisions may already be difficult to reverse.

We judge technological change by one standard

Whether it improves working people's income, security, dignity, and control over their time. People drive to pay rent, care for family, study, manage health needs, supplement another job, or build a livelihood over many years. The ability to decide when to work and when to stop can be as important as the income a driver earns.

We consider robotaxi service beneficial only if its operation improves drivers' work and lives — higher pay for the hours they work, fewer hours without lost income or security, well-paid work for people who want or need it, or other gains in stability, freedom, and opportunity.

A practical test case

Autonomous vehicles operate on public streets, depend on public rules and infrastructure, and produce activity that can be measured. Passenger transportation gives workers, officials, businesses, and researchers a concrete example — before the transition is complete.

Leadership

Project co-leads

Different roles, one project: research and writing, strategy and institutional support, and driver engagement.

RSP

Rayan Semery-Palumbo

Primary author · Fellow, Economic Security Project · Director of Narrative Strategy, One Fair Wage

Led the project's research, policy development, analysis, writing, and production — integrating driver testimony, existing research, submitted proposals, financial modeling, and advisor feedback into the policy framework. He earned a DPhil in Policy and Economics from the University of Oxford, where he was a Rhodes Scholar. His commitment to just worker transitions is personal: his mother was displaced by AI.

AS

Andy Stern

President Emeritus, SEIU · Senior Fellow, Economic Security Project

Advised project strategy, helped build financial and institutional support, recruited contributors and advisors, and connected the project with leaders in labor, government, economics, and technology. He led the Service Employees International Union from 1996 to 2010 and is the author of Raising the Floor: How a Universal Basic Income Can Renew Our Economy and Rebuild the American Dream.

TD

Tash Diallo

Uber & Lyft driver · Veteran organizer · Former National Organizing Director, Independent Drivers Guild

Led the project's driver engagement, bringing experience organizing app-based drivers and working on automation's effects long before this project began. As National Organizing Director of the Independent Drivers Guild, he helped lead campaigns that won in-app tipping and New York City's first minimum-pay standard for app-based drivers.

Contributors

A public call for proposals

The policy framework draws on proposals submitted by researchers, organizers, and policy experts — including James Parrott (The New School), Devesh Kodnani and Emily Lemmerman (MIT), the Predistribution Initiative, Graeme Nuttall OBE and the Institute for the Future of Work, and more than a dozen others. A proposal credit does not imply endorsement of the final framework.

Advisory panel

Twelve independent reviewers

A twelve-member panel of labor leaders, economists, investors, scholars, technology experts, and former public officials reviewed the proposals that moved forward and identified their strengths, weaknesses, and practical risks. To preserve candor, individual comments are not attributed.
